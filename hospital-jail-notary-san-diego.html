Quick answer

Yes, SD Fast Notary sends a mobile notary to hospitals, assisted living facilities, nursing homes, and detention facilities throughout San Diego County, 7 days a week. Call (858) 859-1779 and tell us the facility, room or booking number, the document, and the signer's ID situation.

Hospital and care facility notary

When a loved one is in the hospital, documents like powers of attorney, advance health care directives, and grant deeds often can't wait. We come to the bedside, explain the process calmly, and work around care schedules.

We can meet you at hospitals and care facilities across the county, including Scripps, Sharp, UC San Diego Health, Kaiser Permanente, Palomar Health, and Tri-City Medical Center locations, plus local assisted living and skilled nursing facilities.

Before we arrive

  • Confirm the patient is alert and able to communicate directly with the notary (no translator through a third party).
  • Locate the patient's ID, or arrange credible witnesses.
  • Have the complete document ready, unsigned.
  • For an advance directive in a skilled nursing facility, arrange the required patient advocate or ombudsman witness.

Jail and detention facility notary

We notarize documents for people in custody at San Diego County detention facilities, such as powers of attorney, vehicle releases, and property documents. The inmate ID issued by the facility is acceptable identification while the person is in custody. Each facility has its own visiting process, so call us first and we will coordinate timing and requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Can a notary come to a hospital in San Diego?

Yes. SD Fast Notary visits hospitals across San Diego County 7 days a week. The patient must be alert, able to communicate directly with the notary, and have acceptable ID or credible witnesses.

What if the patient's ID is at home or expired?

An ID that expired within the last five years from issue is still acceptable. Without acceptable ID, two credible witnesses who know the patient, or one known to both patient and notary, can identify them under oath.

Can you notarize for someone in jail in San Diego County?

Yes. We visit San Diego County detention facilities. An inmate ID issued by the facility is acceptable identification while the person is in custody. Each facility sets its own visiting rules, so call us first to coordinate.

Can a notary notarize if the patient is on medication?

The notary must be satisfied the signer is aware and understands what they are signing. If the signer seems confused or unable to communicate, the notary must decline and can return later.
